# AN INFORMATION-THEORETIC APPROACH TO THE ORGANIZATION OF MORPHOSYNTACTIC INFORMATION UNDER FORMAL CONSTRAINT

Leonardo Tornabene — July 2026

## SUMMARY

A formal constraint can be described, within an appropriate representation, as a restriction imposed on the set of possible linguistic sequences; this restriction conditions the distribution of the linguistic choices actually realized. This project aims to verify whether the qualitative distinction between prose and hexameter poetry (subject to a formal constraint in metrical composition) leaves a measurable signature in the predictive organization of morphosyntactic sequences. Universal Dependencies is the reference for the morphosyntactic mapping: the framework of Universal Dependencies uses morphological categories and syntactic dependency relations to describe linguistic structure. The project does not analyse the UD treebanks directly, but a linear and reduced projection of them, which keeps for each word its grammatical category and the type of dependency relation that links it to the word on which it grammatically depends (its head). These sequences will be analysed with a single statistical instrument: the context tree developed by the information theorist Jorma J. Rissanen. It is a model with contexts of variable length, built according to the principle of Minimum Description Length: greater complexity of the model is accepted only if it reduces the overall length of the description of the data. This predictive model makes it possible to take the complexity of language into account while carrying out a rigorous statistical analysis of how knowledge of the past influences that of the future in the two different textual regimes, prose and poetry. The root of the model (the distribution of the symbols without any context), the gain obtained from the context (how many bits per symbol are saved by knowing the recent past of the sequence) and the predictive transfer between regimes (how much the regularities learned in one regime predict the documents of the other) provide three coordinated readings (distributional, sequential and predictive) of the same object.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

**1.1 General problem.** Already in *A Mathematical Theory of Communication* [1] Shannon observes that natural languages can be treated, for specific quantitative problems, as processes that produce discrete sequences of symbols chosen from a finite set. This idealization is not a general theory of language: it does, however, make it possible to formulate precise questions about the distribution, the predictability and the contextual dependence of the observed elements. Within this approximation, a formal constraint can be considered as a restriction acting on the process that produces the sequence. Poetic metre is a particularly clear case of it, because it imposes systematic conditions on the realization of the text while leaving available a wide set of linguistically admissible alternatives.

**1.2 Research question.** The distinction between prose and poetry is qualitatively recognizable on the literary, metrical and perceptual level. The project asks whether this distinction also leaves a quantitative signature in the morphosyntactic organization of texts. The question is not whether a classifier can separate prose and verse, nor whether poetry generically shows greater or lower entropy. The question is more specific: with the texts represented at the same morphosyntactic level and the same predictive model applied uniformly, in which seat of the statistical organization does the possible difference between the hexameter regime and the prose regime emerge? Respectively in the frequency of the symbols, in the informative value of the linear context, or in the transferability of the predictive regularities between the two regimes?

**1.3 Level of analysis.** The chosen level is morphosyntactic, not semantic and not directly prosodic. The choice answers a need for control: lemmas and words incorporate content, theme, genre and lexical tradition; prosodic sequences describe the metre directly, but do not make it possible to establish whether the constraint is accompanied by a reorganization of the grammatical realization. The UPOS categories and the DEPREL relations of Universal Dependencies [2] instead make it possible to abstract, to a controlled extent, from the lexical content while keeping information on the grammatical class of the words and on the function they perform in the dependency structure.

**1.4 Linking hypothesis.** The project does not assume that the metre modifies the grammar of Greek or Latin. It assumes a more limited thesis: a strong metrical constraint can influence the selection and arrangement of the alternatives that the grammar makes available. Word order, choice between competing constructions, use of particles, distribution of the categories, periphrastic forms and morphological solutions can all contribute to satisfying the verse. If such adaptations are systematic, part of their trace can be preserved in the linear succession of the UPOS categories and DEPREL relations. This is the bridge hypothesis that authorizes the statistical analysis; it does not imply that every observed difference is caused by the metre, nor that the chosen representation captures the whole linguistic structure.

**1.5 Operational formulation.** The texts are transformed into sequences of morphosyntactic symbols and analysed with a single family of variable-memory predictive models: a context tree with MDL (Minimum Description Length) selection, in the tradition of Rissanen and in the operational formulation studied by Schürmann and Grassberger [4, 8]. The instrument is read at three levels: the root, which describes the repertoire and the frequency of the symbols; the context gain, which measures how many bits per symbol the linear past makes it possible to save with respect to the root; the inter-regime transfer, which measures how much the regularities learned on one regime predict documents of the same or of the other regime. Greek is the primary contrast; Latin provides a subordinate replication.

## 2. STATE OF THE ART

**2.1 Informational analysis of texts.** The application of information theory to natural languages goes back to Shannon himself [1], but the estimation of the entropy of finite linguistic sequences presents specific difficulties. Texts show dependencies at several scales and a number of configurations that grows rapidly with the length of the context. Estimators based on the direct counting of fixed-order blocks therefore converge slowly and can become unstable. Schürmann and Grassberger [4] tackle the problem explicitly through estimators linked to the code lengths produced by compression algorithms, among them Rissanen's method of contexts. Montemurro and Zanette [5] instead use Lempel–Ziv estimators to measure the contribution of word order in several language families.

**2.2 Quantitative studies of verse.** Poetry has already been the object of informational analysis, above all on the prosodic level. Mansilla and Bush [6] transform the Greek and Latin hexameter into rhythmic symbolic series and use mutual information to describe differences in the complexity of the patterns. Šeļa and Gronas [7] propose the entropy of the intervals between stresses as a continuous measure of rhythmic regularity. These works show that a metrical constraint can be treated quantitatively, but they operate on prosodic alphabets. The present project moves the observation to the morphosyntactic level: it does not measure the metre as such, but verifies whether its presence is accompanied by differences in the organization of the attested grammatical forms.

**2.3 Universal Dependencies as a descriptive reference.** Universal Dependencies is at the same time an annotation infrastructure and a coherent framework for morphosyntactic description comparable across languages [2]. The model takes words as fundamental units, links them through asymmetric binary relations between head and dependent, and types these relations through grammatical labels. The basic trees represent a surface morphosyntactic structure centred on predicates, arguments and modifiers. UPOS describes the grammatical category of the word; DEPREL describes the relation of the token with its head. The project adopts this level not because Universal Dependencies is an exhaustive theory of syntax, but because it offers an explicit, documented, replicable and sufficiently uniform representation for the comparison between Greek and Latin.

**2.4 From structure to statistical observation.** Probabilities and informative measures are not independent of the representation on which they are computed. Greco et al. [21] stress, in a different context and with psycholinguistic aims, that a statistical measure such as surprisal acquires linguistic content only with respect to the model that defines the events and the probabilities. The methodological principle is relevant here too: it is not enough to apply an algorithm to arbitrary symbols; one must specify what linguistic information those symbols preserve. In the present project the UPOS+DEPREL pair does not coincide with the complete syntactic tree. It is a linear and lossy projection, derived from a hierarchical representation: it preserves the grammatical class and the type of dependency, but it does not preserve the identity of the head, the distance of the dependency, the branching, the embedding or the whole topology of the tree. The conclusions will therefore refer to the predictive organization of this representation, not to the grammar as a whole.

**2.5 Models with contexts of variable length.** The context algorithm originates with Rissanen [8]. The statistical framework of variable-length Markov chains is developed by Bühlmann and Wyner [9], while Csiszár and Talata [10] establish consistency results for the estimation of context trees through model selection criteria such as BIC and MDL. The closest linguistic precedent is Galves et al. [11], who use context tree selection to retrieve linguistic rhythm from written texts and distinguish European and Brazilian Portuguese. Their alphabet is rhythmic-accentual and the question concerns the linguistic variety; the present project uses a morphosyntactic alphabet, takes a metrical constraint as the factor of contrast and introduces as central reading the predictive transfer between regimes. Context-Tree Weighting [12], which averages over the models instead of selecting one, is a possible robustness comparator.

**2.6 Positioning in the classical languages.** The data derive from the Ancient Greek and Latin Dependency Treebank of the Perseus project [13], converted into the Universal Dependencies standard. Chen et al. [14] show that the distinction between Latin prose and verse is easily classifiable through enriched part-of-speech features. The simple discriminability of the two regimes is therefore not a sufficient contribution. The problem proposed here is to localize the difference within a predictive instrument: to establish whether it is mainly distributional, whether it concerns the value of the context, or whether it shows itself as regime-specific structure that transfers worse between prose and hexameter. Herrera et al. [15] also show the usefulness and the limits of contrastive descriptions extracted from UD treebanks, recalling the need to control the annotation conventions.

**2.7 Theoretical limit of the statistical model.** The context tree is not proposed as a model of the grammar of natural languages. Chomsky's classical argument [16] against the adequacy of finite-memory Markov models as grammatical descriptions keeps its full validity. The justification of the context tree is instrumental: it provides an adaptive and reproducible procedure to measure the sequential predictability of a discrete representation, comparing all the regimes within the same class of models. No statements are made about linguistic competence, grammaticality or the generative nature of language. Linguistic theory intervenes exclusively to define and delimit the observed level.

**2.8 Conceptual framing.** The reference to the perspective of complex systems is epistemological in character. Anderson [3] observes that quantitative differences of organization can be accompanied by qualitative distinctions at higher descriptive levels. Prose and poetry are therefore treated as different regimes of organization of the same linguistic material, one of which is subject to a strong formal constraint. The project does not interpret the gap as a phase transition or symmetry breaking in the physical sense: no quantity of the design operationalizes such notions.

## 3. RESEARCH HYPOTHESES

**3.1 General hypothesis.** The research hypothesis is that the metrical constraint is accompanied by a statistically detectable reorganization of the distribution of the morphosyntactic realizations within the space of the alternatives admitted by the grammar, and that this reorganization leaves a signature in the predictive organization of the UPOS+DEPREL sequence. No general direction of the effect is assumed: greater constraint does not necessarily mean lower entropy, greater regularity or greater predictability. The direction is specified only when it derives from the definition of the statistic and from the hypothesis of regime-specific structure.

**3.2 Distributional premise.** Before the hypotheses, the two regimes are compared at the most elementary level: the repertoire of the UPOS+DEPREL symbols and their relative frequencies, without using any context; in the context tree this repertoire corresponds to the root. This comparison serves to prepare the reading of the hypotheses that follow. If the two regimes use the same categories with similar frequencies, a possible difference in the following hypotheses will concern the way in which the categories are organized in sequence, and not the available inventory, consistently with the linking hypothesis (Introduction 1.4); markedly different repertoires would instead require greater caution in the interpretation. To give this comparison a summary measure, the distance between the root distributions of the two regimes is quantified by the Jensen–Shannon divergence [22] (Methodology 6.8).

**3.3 Hypothesis H1: sequential reading.** The predictive value of the linear context differs between the two regimes. The confirmatory statistic compares the per-document mean of the context gain, that is the bits per symbol saved by the complete model with respect to the root alone. This statistic will be computed held-out, that is on text never used for training the model (Methodology 6.6). The scores are obtained through leave-one-document-out models: each trained on all the documents except the one under examination, built without consulting the regime labels and at a common training size, so that the scores are fixed properties of the documents. This guarantees the possibility of carrying out a permutation test.

**3.4 Hypothesis H2: predictive reading.** The regularities learned within a regime predict documents of the same regime better than documents of the other. The confirmatory statistic is the transfer asymmetry: for each document, the cross-entropy obtained with the model trained on the document's own regime is subtracted from the cross-entropy obtained with the model trained on the other regime, at equal quantity of training data. A positive mean value indicates the presence of a regime-specific predictive structure. A secondary but possible reading is the mean depth of the selected context, which is however more sensitive to the parameters of the instrument.

**3.5 Hypothesis H3: Latin replication.** The signs of the main effects found in Greek replicate qualitatively in Latin, with their confidence intervals and with the quantities computed with respect to Greek subsamples of the same size, within the limits imposed by the size and composition of the corpus. The Latin replication is subordinate by construction: it does not enter the family of confirmatory tests and is not interpreted as a symmetric comparison between languages.

**3.6 Confirmatory family.** The family of confirmatory tests comprises only the measures pertaining to hypotheses H1 and H2. The multiplicity of the tests is controlled through the Holm–Bonferroni procedure [17], which adjusts the significance thresholds to the joint evaluation of several hypotheses, with α = 0.05. This restricted family consolidates an earlier formulation of the project, which set several independent classical measures side by side; the consolidation on a single instrument and the definition of the confirmatory statistics precede any computation on the real data, so that no inferential choice is oriented by the preliminary exploration of the data.

## 4. RATIONALE OF THE CASE STUDY

**4.1 Why the morphosyntactic level.** The morphosyntactic level is a controlled compromise between proximity to linguistic structure and statistical comparability. A lexical analysis would be strongly exposed to theme, author, genre and formulaic tradition; a purely metrical analysis would directly measure the constraint already known. UPOS and DEPREL make it possible to observe the distribution and succession of grammatical categories and functions, reducing the impact of lexical identity. The project does not consider this level as the complete representation of the language, but as a defined and replicable observational plane on which to formulate a precise quantitative question.

**4.2 Why Universal Dependencies.** Universal Dependencies is chosen for three reasons. First, it makes the surface morphosyntactic structure explicit through words, heads and typed dependencies. Second, it applies a shared scheme to different languages, making it possible to use the same encoding function in Greek and Latin without building an ad hoc mapping between two annotation systems. Third, the Perseus treebanks provide texts in the original language, already segmented and annotated, belonging both to poetry and to prose. The common scheme does not imply that the alphabets empirically realized in the two languages are identical, nor that every label has perfect functional equivalence: such asymmetries are treated as limits and controlled through audits.

**4.3 Why prose and poetry.** The comparison starts from a culturally and philologically established distinction, but it does not assume that this category automatically coincides with a statistical separation. The distinction is put to the test. Classical prose is used as the regime less directly constrained by metrical structure; the hexameter as the regime subject to a strong quantitative constraint. The differences found remain corpus associations and are not attributed causally to the metre without further controls.

**4.4 Why the Greek and Latin hexameter.** The hexameter is a recognizable and historically continuous metrical-quantitative form, although with different linguistic, chronological and authorial realizations [6]. Its presence in both traditions makes it possible to test the method on two inflectional languages comparable in history and structure, but not identical. Greek offers a corpus large enough for the primary contrast; Latin allows a cautious replication. So-called contemporary prose is operationalized as classical prose: strict contemporaneity with archaic epic is not available, and this impossibility is an explicit confounder.

**4.5 Scope of the bridge hypothesis.** The bridge hypothesis does not require a specific theory of the interface between metre and syntax. It is enough that the metrical constraint redistributes, even indirectly, the probability of linguistic solutions already admitted: relative positions, categories, relations and sequences of functions. The project measures the aggregate outcome of these choices in the observed representation. It does not attempt to derive the constructions from the metre, to identify an autonomous poetic grammar or to explain the competence of the speakers.

## 5. CORPUS AND COMPUTATIONAL TOOLS

**5.1 Structure of the data.** The data are the treebanks UD_Ancient_Greek-Perseus and UD_Latin-Perseus, release v2.18 [18, 19], conversions into the Universal Dependencies standard of the Ancient Greek and Latin Dependency Treebank [13]. Every sentence is represented in the CoNLL-U format. For each syntactic word there are available, among other fields, form, lemma, UPOS category, morphological features, index of the head and DEPREL relation. The project uses UPOS and the basic dependency relation; the other information is kept for audit and traceability, but does not enter the primary alphabet.

**5.2 Observation model.** Let T be a sentence annotated as a dependency tree and let v₁,…,vₙ be its words in surface order. To each retained word the symbol

φ(vᵢ) = UPOS(vᵢ):DEPREL-base(vᵢ)

is associated. The observed sequence is therefore

φ(T) = [φ(v₁),…,φ(vₙ)].

This function preserves the linear order, the grammatical category and the type of the relation of the token with its head. It does not preserve the identity of the head, the direction and length of the dependency, the branching, the relations between dependencies or the constituency structure. The sequence is therefore defined as a linear, coarse-grained and dependency-informed representation of the morphosyntactic realization. This is the object of the statistical model.

**5.3 Greek corpus.** The Greek treebank comprises 13,919 sentences and 202,989 tokens [18]. The primary contrast opposes five hexameter documents — Iliad, Theogony, Works and Days, Shield of Heracles, Hymn to Demeter — to six documents of classical prose — Herodotus, Thucydides and four speeches of Lysias. The corpus also comprises twelve tragedies, seven by Aeschylus and five by Sophocles, used as an exploratory set of non-hexameter verse, and six authors of post-classical prose, used as a chronological control. The operational categories distinguish hexameter, classical prose, post-classical prose, other verse and excluded documents. The assignments are verified through a documentary audit before the analysis.

**5.4 Latin corpus.** The Latin treebank comprises 2,273 sentences, 28,868 tokens and 29,223 syntactic words, with 355 multiword tokens, among them clitic univerbations such as -que and -ue, which are expanded [19]. The hexameter regime comprises the Aeneid and the Metamorphoses. Prose brings together, for reasons of numbers, classical and post-classical texts; Propertius and Phaedrus are the non-hexameter verse; the Vulgate is excluded. This makes a total of 6 prose documents and 2 poetry documents; this asymmetry, together with the reduced volumes of the corpus and the heterogeneity of the genres, imposes a subordinate use of Latin.

**5.5 Primary alphabet.** The alphabet keeps

12 UPOS: ADJ, ADP, ADV, AUX, CCONJ, DET, NOUN, NUM, PART, PRON, SCONJ, VERB

and 23 DEPREL: root, nsubj, csubj, obj, iobj, ccomp, xcomp, obl, advcl, advmod, acl, amod, appos, det, nummod, nmod, case, mark, aux, cop, cc, conj, parataxis.

The subtypes of the relations are brought back to the basic relation; PROPN is brought back to NOUN in Latin, which distinguishes proper nouns. Punctuation, X, interjections and symbols are excluded. The residual relations follow a controlled policy: elimination in the primary analysis and merging into a single residual category in the sensitivity analysis. If an excluded relation exceeds an established minimum threshold of the raw tokens of a regime, both policies are reported with equal prominence. The maximum theoretical alphabet has 276 symbols; the effective one is the set of symbols observed after the audit.

**5.6 UPOS-only control and boundaries.** All the main statistics are replicated on an alphabet made of the UPOS alone. This control separates the results robust to grammatical categorization alone from those dependent on the dependency relations, whose annotation can be noisier in poetic texts and in non-canonical order [14, 15]. In the primary analysis the contexts are reset at the start of every sentence; a sensitivity analysis introduces an explicit boundary symbol. Verse boundaries are not encoded in the treebanks and are not reconstructed in the primary design.

**5.7 Provenance and licence.** The treebank release, the commits and the file hashes are recorded; the raw data remain unchanged. The CC BY-NC-SA 2.5 licence allows research use but not the indiscriminate redistribution of the data; code, configurations and derived results are published.

## 6. METHODOLOGY

**6.1 Sequences and units of analysis.** For each document the sentences are ordered according to the corpus and transformed into sequences of symbols; the eliminations close the gaps and, in the primary analysis, the contexts do not cross sentence boundaries (the alternative policy, with an explicit boundary symbol, is part of the sensitivity plan, Methodology 6.12). The chosen unit of analysis is the document: not the token, not the sentence, not an arbitrary portion of text. Blocks of comparable length may be used exclusively for descriptive plots within the documents; they are not independent statistical replicates.

**6.2 The instrument: the variable-memory context tree.** A context tree assigns to the next symbol a probability distribution over the alphabet, conditioned on a suffix of the past of variable length [9]. The model is built on the suffixes actually observed up to a maximum depth decided before any confirmatory analysis (Methodology 6.12). For each context s a prequential predictor computes the probabilities before updating the counts. To prevent the trivial estimate that would assign zero probability to every symbol not yet encountered, a Bayesian smoothing with β = 0.5 is adopted, which corresponds to the Krichevsky–Trofimov estimate discussed by Schürmann and Grassberger [4]. For every context, the code length assigned by the node is compared with the one its parent would have assigned to the same events; the advantage of the context is:

Δ(s) = L_parent(s) − L_own(s).

The deeper context is selected only if Δ(s) > 0 and it has at least k_min = 2 observations. This rule operates along the whole path of the suffixes: a context can be used only if each of its intermediate prefixes in turn satisfies both conditions, and the algorithm stops at the first level that does not satisfy them. No deep context is thus accepted by virtue of an isolated fluctuation, if the levels that lead to it do not each justify their own complexity. The selection follows the MDL (Minimum Description Length) principle: greater complexity is accepted only when it reduces the overall length of the description, and prequential coding incorporates by itself the cost of the model, without separate additive penalties. The correspondence between the concrete implementation and the formulation of Schürmann and Grassberger [4] is validated explicitly before any analysis (Methodology 6.12).

**6.3 The measured quantity: code lengths and cross-entropy.** The model is not interpreted as a real source of the language: code lengths are operational quantities, produced by a class of predictors applied uniformly to all the regimes. The comparison between a document and a predictive model is expressed by the held-out cross-entropy: for a document d and a frozen model M,

CE(d | M) = −(1/N_d) ∑ₜ log q_M(xₜ | cₜ),

where q_M is the probability that M assigns to the observed symbol in position t given the available context cₜ, and N_d is the number of positions evaluated; the logarithm is in base 2 and the unit is the bit per symbol [20]. Every evaluation happens with the tree frozen, on material the model has not seen in training. When the observed past does not correspond to any context of the frozen tree, the evaluation falls back on the deepest context available along the path of the suffixes and, in the last resort, on the root, which is always defined; the Bayesian smoothing also guarantees that no symbol receives zero probability, not even when it was never observed in training. Every position therefore admits a finite and well-defined code length. It is important to note that the comparison does not require M to be an adequate description of the real grammar: it requires that the same instrument be validated and applied with the same rules to all the documents.

**6.4 Why a single instrument.** The classical fixed-order measures are already contained in the model, with their limitations attenuated: the root induces a zero-order distribution, from which a Shannon entropy can be derived as an internal diagnostic. The depth-1 nodes can instead be traced back to conditional distributions of order 1, from which a reading of the conditional entropy. Finally the comparison between root and contexts quantifies directly the sequential gain (Methodology 6.7). A distinction must however be kept: the entropy of an estimated distribution, the cross-entropy computed on material never seen in training and the prequential code length are different quantities, and what the project measures and compares are only the second and the third (Methodology 6.3). Computing entropy, conditional entropy and mutual information separately as independent results would increase the multiplicity, without tackling the rigidity and sparsity proper to these same fixed-order estimates, which the variable-context model instead makes it possible to attenuate. The conceptual objection to finite-memory models as descriptions of the grammar (Chomsky [16]) instead remains intact, in the terms set out in 2.7 and taken up in 8.5.

**6.5 Why not compare the trees.** The context tree is used as a measuring instrument, not as an object of study: what is compared is not the models, but the code lengths that one and the same procedure, applied with the same rules to all the documents, assigns to data never seen in training. The apparently more direct option would be to fit a tree to each document and compare the selected structures (which contexts, at which depth). It is however to be avoided for three converging reasons.

The first is that the structure of a tree selected with MDL depends on the quantity of data even before the organization of the source: a node is accepted only when its counts justify its complexity, and with little text few contexts would reach the necessary support. Two documents analysed with the same process, but of very different size, would produce systematically different trees by construction, and the comparison between structures would above all measure the length of the texts; the consistency results for the selection of context trees [10] are asymptotic, and the document sizes at stake here are far from them.

The second is that the correspondence between trees and predictive distributions is many-to-one: structurally different trees can encode almost identical predictions, and a minimal fluctuation in the counts can overturn the selection of a node whose advantage is close to the threshold. The code lengths are the stable datum; the identity of the selected nodes is a fragile counterpart of it. The MDL model considers the cost of describing the data, not the particular model that realizes it.

The third reason is inferential: a comparison between structures would require quantifying a distance between trees: such a measure is hard to define and does not represent an exchangeable quantity on which to base an exact test; the cross-entropies are instead scalars in bits per symbol, with the structure of exchangeability described in the following paragraph.

**6.6 The common-model strategy and exchangeability.** To overcome these problems the project adopts a common-model strategy. For each document a model is built using all the others: the same instrument (same alphabet, same maximum depth, same smoothing, same selection rule) is rebuilt each time with the available material, excluding the document under examination. The model thus obtained is frozen and asked to predict precisely the excluded document: the evaluation is therefore genuinely held-out, that is on data the model has never seen. The reason is the honesty of the measure: a model evaluated on its own training material does not reveal how predictable a text is, but how much it has memorized it, and it would systematically reward the longer and more repetitive documents; asking the model to predict symbols never seen allows a real operational quantification of "how much the context helps".

For the context gain the instrument is trained on the undifferentiated set of the other documents, poetry and prose together, without ever consulting the regime labels. The common model thus acts as a yardstick: it encodes the sequential regularities of the corpus, and each document is queried with respect to that average. Since the documents have very different lengths, excluding a poem would remove from the training much more material than excluding a speech, and the score would end up also reflecting the quantity of data left, not only the evaluated document. To neutralize this effect, every training set is brought to the same common size T, sampling whole sentences uniformly and without consulting the labels, and repeating the draw over several seeds with averaging of the scores. T is defined as the minimum quantity of material available among all the training conditions of the two protocols, sequential and predictive (Methodology 6.7 and 6.8). The equality of size eliminates the quantity of training material as a possible systematic source of deviation in the results. Moreover the blindness to the labels guarantees the validity of the permutation test, for the reason that follows.

A statistical test does not verify the research hypothesis directly: it measures how surprising the observed data would be if there were no difference between the regimes. One must therefore be able to represent exactly this null scenario. If the regimes do not differ, the labels "hexameter" and "prose" are contingent: the scores of the documents would be what they are for reasons unrelated to the regime, and any reassignment of the labels would be as plausible as the real one; this is what is meant by saying that, under the null hypothesis, the labels are exchangeable. The permutation test then considers all the 462 possible recombinations of the eleven documents into five and six (five for poetry, six for prose), computes for each the difference between the means of the two groups and places the really observed value within this distribution. The resulting p-value is exact, without asymptotic approximations or distributional assumptions: an indispensable requirement with only eleven documents, where no parametric test would be defensible. The enumeration however makes sense under one condition only: that the scores be fixed properties of the documents, computed once and valid under every reassignment. This is exactly what the blind construction guarantees. If instead the scores were produced by regime-specific models (the poems measured by a poetry model, the prose texts by a prose model), each score would carry the imprint of the label received through the very way it was computed, before and independently of any real difference; permuting the labels while keeping the scores fixed would no longer represent the null scenario, and the number obtained would have the form of a p-value without its meaning. The blind construction guarantees that the scores are invariant with respect to any later reassignment of the labels, and can therefore be subjected to permutation. The exactness of the test however remains conditional on a further assumption, which the construction cannot provide by itself: that under the null hypothesis the eleven documents be actually exchangeable between the two groups. The corpus contains several works by the same author and strong symmetries between regime, author, epoch and genre; for this reason the primary analysis is accompanied by a conservative replication at the author level (Methodology 6.11), which treats the authorial blocks as units instead of the single documents

In the predictive transfer (which asks whether no regime has a structure of its own) the two models trained on the same quantity of material should be interchangeable for each document, and the sign of the difference would be arbitrary. Therefore the simplest formulation of a statistical test on the results would be the sign-flip (Methodology 6.11). The simultaneous and independent inversion of the signs on all the documents however requires the joint symmetry of the signs under the null hypothesis, which does not follow automatically from the construction, because the models that produce the scores share part of the training material. This assumption must be formulated explicitly, and its adequacy is submitted to statistical supervision (The Role of the Supervisor 11.1).

In both cases the logic of the design is the same: build the measures so that the properties required by the tests are, as far as possible, guaranteed by the construction, declaring explicitly as an assumption what the construction cannot guarantee.

**6.7 H1 context gain (sequential reading).** For each document d a model is fitted on the set of the other documents (at the common size T), according to the label-blind protocol set out in Methodology 6.6. On the excluded document the cross-entropy of the root alone and that of the complete model are computed; the mean gain is

G(d) = CE_root(d) − CE_complete(d),

in bits per symbol: it measures how much knowledge of the recent past reduces the coding cost with respect to the marginal distribution of the symbols alone. A positive value represents a code saving due to the context; a negative value indicates that, on the document held out from training, the contextual structure learned from the other documents does not improve the prediction with respect to the root alone. To prevent editorial differences in the segmentation of the sentences from influencing the quantity of available context, G(d) is computed primarily only on the positions preceded, within the same sentence, by a minimum number of symbols; the removal of this restriction is a variation of the sensitivity plan (Methodology 6.12). The confirmatory statistic is the difference between the mean of G(d) in the hexameter documents and the mean in the documents of classical prose. Since the vector of the scores is built without using the labels, it remains unchanged under their reassignment. The permutation test is therefore applicable to the vector of the scores, on condition of accepting the assumption of exchangeability of the documents under the null hypothesis (Methodology 6.6): the 462 possible assignments of the eleven documents to the two groups.

**6.8 Root distributions.** Since the gain is defined as a saving with respect to the root, the distributions of the symbols in the two regimes will also be inspected as an interpretive control (Research Hypotheses 3.2): similar repertoires accompanied by systematically different gains indicate a difference in the sequential organization, not in the inventory of the categories used, consistently with the bridge hypothesis according to which the metrical constraint polarizes the selection between grammatically available alternatives (Introduction 1.4, Rationale of the Case Study 4.5). The comparison is summarized by a synthetic measure: the Jensen–Shannon divergence [22] between the root distributions of the reference models of the two regimes, computed with the same smoothing on the frozen alphabet. This quantity quantifies in a symmetric and rigorous way the distance between two marginal distributions of symbols; it is defined as

JSD(P, Q) = ½ D(P ∥ M) + ½ D(Q ∥ M), with M = ½ (P + Q),

where D is the Kullback–Leibler divergence with logarithms in base 2 [20, 22]; the value lies between 0, for identical distributions, and 1 bit, for distributions without symbols in common. This reading, together with the inspection of the frequencies, is declaredly descriptive in character.

**6.9 H2: transfer between regimes (predictive reading).** For each document d two leave-one-document-out models are built: one on the regime it belongs to, excluding d, and one on the other regime. The two training pools are subsampled by whole sentences to the common size T (Methodology 6.6); the procedure is repeated over several subsampling seeds and the scores are averaged over the seeds. The transfer asymmetry is

ΔCE(d) = CE_other(d) − CE_own(d).

The confirmatory statistic is the mean of ΔCE(d) over the documents. The hypothesis is that this mean is positive: if regime-specific regularities exist, the model of the document's own regime must assign a smaller code length. With paired training, under the null hypothesis and under the assumption of symmetry of the signs (Methodology 6.6), the test is the exact sign-flip: the enumeration of the 2¹¹ = 2048 possible sign configurations.

**6.10 Secondary statistic: the selected depth.** For each document the mean depth of the context actually used is computed, on the same restricted positions as the gain and under the same label-blind protocol; the means of the two regimes are compared through the same exact permutation. It is a secondary statistic because it depends more directly on the maximum depth, the minimum support and the selection rule.

**6.11 Multiplicity, effects and uncertainty.** The Holm–Bonferroni procedure [17] controls the family of the two confirmatory statistics (H1, context gain; H2, predictive transfer) at α = 0.05, with ordered thresholds 0.025 and 0.05. The minimum exact p-values reachable by the primary design (1/2048 ≈ 0.00049 for the sign-flip, 1/462 ≈ 0.0022 for the permutation of the labels) are below both thresholds: family significance is therefore reachable. The effects are reported in the natural units of the statistics, that is bits per symbol. The confidence intervals are obtained through a document bootstrap stratified by regime. Given the small numbers, the intervals must be interpreted with caution and accompanied by visualizations of the single documents. The small numbers finally require a control of authorial dependence. Since the corpus contains several works by the same author, the whole confirmatory analysis is replicated in conservative form treating as units not the documents but the authorial blocks. For Greek these are three hexameter blocks (the Homeric poem, the Hesiodic group, the anonymous hymn) and three prose blocks (Herodotus, Thucydides, the group of the speeches of Lysias). With six blocks the exact enumerations count 20 assignments for the permutation of the labels and 64 configurations for the sign-flip: the minimum reachable p-values, respectively 0.10 and about 0.016, are declared in advance and show that this replication cannot reach family significance. Its role is not confirmatory: it is to verify that the sign and the order of magnitude of the effects survive when the dependence between documents of the same author is removed by construction.

**6.12 Validation of the instrument.** Before the analysis of the real data, the implementation must pass tests on synthetic sources with known analytical results: a uniform independent source; a deterministic cycle; an order-1 Markov chain with known rate; an order-2 process built so that an order-1 model cannot reach the optimal cross-entropy. The tests verify the predict-then-update order, the normalization of the probabilities, the selection of the depth, the correct handling of the sentence boundaries, the absence of leakage and the invariance of the scores of the common protocol with respect to a preliminary permutation of the labels.

**6.13 Freeze and sensitivity plan.** The audit of the corpus precedes the fitting of any model to the real data. After the audit, the register of the documents, the observed alphabet, the exclusion policies, the common size T and the number of seeds S are fixed (Methodology 6.6, 6.8); the definitions of the two confirmatory statistics, the protocols and the inferential plan are then frozen before the descriptive models. The sensitivity plan comprises thirteen pre-specified configurations: a factorial block that crosses three alphabet policies (UD relations with elimination of the residual ones; UD relations with a single residual category; UPOS only) and two sentence-boundary policies (resetting of the context; explicit boundary symbol), plus seven one-at-a-time variations around the primary configuration: three alternative values of the smoothing, two of the maximum depth, the alternative selection rule and the removal of the position restriction. The thirteen configurations do not all have the same role. The seven one-at-a-time variations modify parameters of the instrument leaving the observed object unchanged: on these, together with the primary configuration, the stability of the sign of the effect is required for a substantive conclusion to be formulated; an instability within this block would itself be a result, and would be reported as such. The cells of the factorial block that modify the alphabet or boundary policy instead change the observed representation: eliminating the dependency relations, or introducing an explicit boundary symbol, is not a numerical perturbation but a choice about which information to observe. Their outcomes are therefore reported and interpreted, not used as a condition of validity: an effect present with the complete alphabet and absent with the UPOS alone, for example, would localize the signature in the dependency information, and would be an interpretable result, not a defect of robustness. Every primary conclusion remains referred, explicitly, to the primary representation.

**6.14 H3: Latin replication and control of size.** Latin uses the same instrument and the same protocols, adapted to its own numbers. The sign-flip has 2⁸ = 256 configurations and the permutation of the labels 28 assignments. Given the small volume available the results cannot be other than qualitative, they do not enter the Holm family, and the replication hypothesis is by design subordinate to the Greek results. To distinguish linguistic differences from sample effects, a predefined number of Greek subsamples will have to be brought to the size of Latin, and the Latin values will be localized with respect to the distribution obtained from these subsamples.

## 7. COMPUTATIONAL ASPECTS

**7.1 Pipeline.** The pipeline will be implemented in Python 3.12 through standard libraries for data handling, numerical computation, inference and visualization. The CoNLL-U files will be acquired, validated and transformed into document registers; the alphabet mapping function will produce a symbol for every retained token. The sequences, the models, the per-document scores and the figures will be saved in open formats and accompanied by metadata.

**7.2 Reproducibility.** Every result must be reconstructible from the version of the data, the configuration, the commit of the code and the random seed. The software dependencies will be frozen; the raw data will remain immutable and no derived file will be overwritten without authorization. The automatic tests will cover both the pipeline and the mathematical identities of the instrument. The public availability of the code and of the configurations will make it possible to replicate the analysis without redistributing the treebanks.

**7.3 Resources.** The model requires no GPU or specialist infrastructure. The complexity is compatible with an ordinary laptop; the overall cost derives above all from the repetition of the fits in the leave-one-out protocols and in the sensitivity plan. The feasibility in time will be verified through profiling on synthetic data before the real analysis.

**7.4 Work plan.** The planned phases are: preparation of the environment and of the basic tests; acquisition and audit of the corpus; freeze of the confirmatory plan; implementation and validation of the context tree; construction of the reference models; computation of the confirmatory statistics; Latin replication; sensitivity analysis; linguistic interpretation of the contexts and finally writing.

## 8. POTENTIAL LIMITATIONS

**8.1 Size and granularity of the corpus.** The primary Greek contrast comprises eleven documents and an even smaller number of independent authors. The power is therefore limited and intra-author dependence cannot be completely eliminated. The conservative replication at the author level (Methodology 6.11) moves in this direction and also verifies whether the sign and the order of magnitude of the effects persist when the unit of analysis is raised to the authorial block.

**8.2 Philological and historical confounders.** Metre, genre, epoch, dialect, author and textual tradition are not orthogonal. The primary Greek hexameter is largely archaic and Ionic-epic, while the prose is Ionic or Attic and chronologically later. In Latin, epic, historiography, oratory, biography and the novel are represented unevenly. The design names these confounders, uses tragedy and post-classical prose as exploratory controls and forbids direct causal attribution to the metre.

**8.3 Limits of the representation.** UPOS+DEPREL is a lossy projection. It does not represent the complete tree, the distance of the dependencies, the relative order between head and dependent as an explicit feature, the morphological features, the lemma or the semantics. A null result may indicate the absence of differences at the observed level but would not necessarily imply the absence of linguistic differences. A positive result concerns the adopted representation and does not authorize statements about overall syntactic complexity.

**8.4 Quality and conventions of the annotation.** The treebanks combine manual annotation, automatic tools and conversions between schemes. The non-canonical order of poetry can make the assignment of dependencies more difficult; some conventions vary between treebanks and languages [14, 15]. Per-document audits, the UPOS-only control, exclusion policies and sensitivity analysis reduce the risk, but do not eliminate it.

**8.5 Misspecification of the model.** The context tree is linear and has finite memory. It does not represent recursion, hierarchy or non-local dependencies as such. Chomsky's objection [16] prevents interpreting it as a grammar or a generative model of the language. The project uses the model as a comparative coding instrument; if the signature depends on properties not accessible to the linear projection, the instrument might not detect it.

**8.6 Sentence and verse boundaries.** The segmentation into sentences is partly editorial and can differ between regimes. The resetting of the context at the start of the sentence is controlled through the position restriction and the sensitivity with a boundary symbol. Verse boundaries, directly relevant to the metrical constraint, are not available in the format used; their absence limits the interpretation and prevents localizing the effects with respect to the structure of the verse.

**8.7 Limits of Latin.** The Latin corpus is small, heterogeneous and has only two hexameter documents. The absence of the article, the different distribution of particles and the annotation differences make a direct numerical comparison with Greek improper. The Latin replication therefore remains qualitative and is read with respect to Greek subsamples of the same size.

**8.8 Scope of the conclusions.** The project is a case study on specific treebanks and specific traditions. It does not generalize automatically to other languages, other metres or poetry in general. It does not identify a universal law of formal constraints and does not define a statistical theory of poetic language. It verifies whether a particular level of representation and a particular instrument reveal a reproducible difference in the corpus considered.

## 9. EXPECTED RESULTS

**9.1 Main objective.** The main result is the localization of the possible signature of the hexameter. A difference limited to the root would indicate above all a different repertoire or a different frequency of the morphosyntactic categories. A difference in the context gain (H1) would indicate that the linear context acquires a different informative value in the two regimes. A difference in the predictive transfer (H2) would indicate that the predictive regularities within a regime transfer less effectively to the other regime. The quantities will be expressed in bits per symbol and accompanied by intervals and per-document scores.

**9.2 Interpretation of the outcomes.** All the outcomes are informative. A positive outcome on both hypotheses would identify a signature both different in the quantity of contextual information (H1) and regime-specific (H2). A positive outcome on H2 only would suggest different predictive regularities but with a similar mean gain; a positive outcome on H1 only would suggest a different dependence on the context without a strong transfer asymmetry. An exclusively distributional difference would confine the effect to the root. A general null outcome, after validation and sensitivity, would indicate that the UPOS+DEPREL projection and the context tree do not stably distinguish the regimes in this corpus.

**9.3 Discipline of the statements.** The admitted formulation will be: «in this corpus, the hexameter regime differs from classical prose in reading X by an effect of Y bits per symbol, with its confidence interval». It will not be stated that the metre causes the difference, that poetry has a distinct grammar or that the depth of the context tree coincides with syntactic depth or cognitive memory. The selected contexts will be described as linear sequences of morphosyntactic labels and traced back to the original sentences before any linguistic interpretation.

**9.4 Planned outputs.** The outputs will comprise: register of the corpus and audit of the alphabet; root distributions and Jensen–Shannon divergence between the regimes, with the contributions per symbol; matrix of the cross-entropies; individual and mean values of the transfer asymmetry ΔCE and of the context gain G; profiles of the gain with respect to the available context; distributions of the depths; learning curves; stability over the sensitivity configurations; a descriptive lexicon of the contexts that produce the greatest code saving. The reading of the contexts will have an interpretive, not confirmatory, function.

## 10. FUTURE PROSPECTS

**10.1 Verse boundaries and richer representations.** A first extension consists in realigning the treebanks to the metrical editions to reintroduce the verse boundaries and verify whether the effects concentrate in specific positions of the hexameter. Representations that preserve direction, distance or configurations of dependency could be compared with the current projection, but they would be a distinct enlargement of the project and would require new hypotheses and new controls.

**10.2 Robustness of the model.** Context-Tree Weighting [12] can be used as a comparator to establish whether the conclusions depend on the MDL selection rule. Further universal coding methods could be introduced only as methodological replications, avoiding rebuilding a battery of heterogeneous measures.

**10.3 Enlargement of the corpora.** A larger Latin corpus would allow a comparison by period and genre. The introduction of other metres, of tragedy in a more controlled form or of different poetic traditions would make it possible to distinguish a signature of the hexameter from a more general signature of verse.

**10.4 Formal constraints and symbolic sources.** If the method shows stability, it could be used to compare other linguistic regimes subject to constraint. The potential contribution remains methodological: to provide a procedure to localize where a qualitative distinction shows itself in the predictive organization of a symbolic sequence. Any extension beyond linguistics will have to be verified case by case.

## 11. THE ROLE OF THE SUPERVISOR

**11.1 Mathematical-computational supervision.** Mathematical-computational supervision is necessary to verify the formulation of the context tree, the correct prequential implementation, the MDL selection, the held-out protocols and the validity of the exact inference. The supervision will also include the evaluation of the consistency between statistics, hypotheses and interpretation of the code lengths.

**11.2 Linguistic supervision.** Linguistic supervision is necessary to verify the correct use of Universal Dependencies, the composition of the regimes, the choices of the alphabet and the interpretation of the selected contexts. The contribution of the linguistic supervision will be limited to verifying the correctness of the adopted representation, the limits of the projection and the correspondence between statistical results and the linguistic level actually observed.

**11.3 Philological supervision.** Philological competence, possibly integrated into the linguistic supervision, is useful to check the attribution of the texts, the metrical classification, the chronological and genre comparability and the peculiarities of Greek and Latin poetic language. These checks are essential to distinguish the results of the instrument from problems of the corpus.
